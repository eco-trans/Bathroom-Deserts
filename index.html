THE CITYWIDE PATTERN

A nearby dot on a map is not the same as dependable access.

The model combines routed walking distance, opening hours, pedestrian activity, and competition for facilities. It reveals where nominal supply disappears once time and user needs are considered.

INTERPRETING THE RESULTS

Bathroom access depends on who, where, and when you are

01

Time makes deserts move

A road can be served in the morning and lose access later as demand changes or facilities close. PM patterns diverged most from the weekly classification.

Planning response

Extend hours or staffing where time-specific gaps—not permanent absence—drive the shortfall.

02

Conditional supply is fragile

Adding semi-public restrooms more than tripled mean tract access, yet made 62.1% of modeled accessibility dependent on businesses and other conditional spaces.

Planning response

Use enforceable no-purchase agreements, minimum hours, clear signage, and reporting requirements.

03

Eligibility reshapes the map

Confirmed all-gender facilities retain only 9.5% of baseline access. Physical-access gains often rely on records whose status is unknown rather than verified.

Planning response

Audit and upgrade entrances, fixtures, transfer space, signage, and all-gender designation.

04

A backup is part of access

After one failed attempt, a person may face a long second walk or no independent alternative—especially when eligible options are scarce.

Planning response

Plan for redundancy, not only proximity, and prioritize dependable facilities in persistent zero-access areas.

METHOD

From restroom records to road-level access.

Each road score combines which facilities are open, how far away they are on the pedestrian network, and how much nearby pedestrian demand they serve.

01Build the facility inventory

Combine 972 public and 1,888 semi-public restroom candidates, including recorded hours, entry conditions, and accessibility attributes.

02Model six periods

Estimate access for weekday and weekend morning, midday, and evening windows using matching facility availability and pedestrian activity.

03Route an 800 m catchment

Measure walking distance through the pedestrian network and sample 25 positions along every modeled road segment.

04Calculate E2SFCA access

Divide each facility’s supply by surrounding demand, apply Gaussian distance decay, and sum the resulting opportunities for each road.

05Identify bathroom deserts

Classify roads with zero access or scores in the lowest 20% of positive values for each time window.
